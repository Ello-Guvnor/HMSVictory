| D21 | 2026-10-09 | Hunt-and-target strategy (`huntTargetShot` in `src/opponent.js`), replacing the random stand-in as the default. It receives only its own earlier shots as `{ row, col, result }` (miss / hit / sunk), the same data the random stand-in got. It is not told which ship sank or how long it was (changed by D28: it is now told the sunk ship's length). **Hunt:** random untried checkerboard square (every ship is 2+ long, so each covers one); once those run out, squares next to any earlier hit come first. **Target:** after a hit, fire at its untried neighbours; once two hits line up, fire at the ends of that line. After a sinking, the straight run of hits through the sinking shot counts as that ship; any other hits keep being chased. Tradeoff: when ships touch end to end, that guess can count too many hits as sunk (fixed by D28, see BUGS.md B11). In measurement, before the "next to earlier hits" rule, 24 of 1,000 simulated games took over 80 shots (worst 100); with it, the worst was 75. Measured over 1,000 seeded random fleets: hunt-and-target averaged 52.6 shots to sink the whole fleet (median 53, range 28–75); the random stand-in averaged 95.4 (median 97). | Proposed (Devin) |

| D28 | 2026-10-10 | Touching ships (B11, at Alex's request to fix the D21 limitation using "only information available through its shots and the game's established feedback"): when the computer sinks a ship, `src/battle.js` also passes `sunkLength`.
